
1. Meaning of "equivariant"

Let G be a finite group. C a category.

When there is an action of G on C there is a categorical notation of G-equivariant objects (objects of C + isomorphism data) denoted C^G.

For example consider the case when G acts trivially on C. Then C^G can be thought as objects of C equiped with G action so that if C=k-v.s. C^G=Rep_k(G)

Normally when one imagine sets or objects V with trivial G-action, one expects V^G=V. 2-cat phenomena.

Continuing the case where G acts trivially. There is a functor from C to C^G that sends Y to Y + trivial G-action.

Categorical quotient is defined as a left adjoint to this. (when exists)

This works for C=sch too. for affine schemes, same as taking the ring of G-invariants. Initial G-invariant map.

There is a concept called quotient stack. Which is different from the scheme quotient. Notation of former : [X/G] latter X/G

Way of thinking : quotient stack establishes equvariant geometry. ex) G-equivariant V.B on * = V.B on [*/G]. remembers stablizers.

ex) */G is just *, [*/G] is classifying stack of G torsors.

ex) if G is free and proper the two are the same. (stablizers are all trivial)
